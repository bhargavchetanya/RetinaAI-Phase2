RetinaAI

Detects diabetic retinopathy in a retina photo and grades how severe it is. Five models are compared: a 5-layer CNN trained from scratch as the baseline, and 4 models pre-trained on ImageNet (ResNet-18, EfficientNet-B0, DenseNet-121, MobileNetV3-Large) fine-tuned with transfer learning. All are trained and tested on the APTOS 2019 dataset (3,662 photos graded by doctors).

Training graphs

The 4 pre-trained models

ResNet-18
2015 · 11.2 M weights

Residual blocks: a skip connection adds each block's input to its output, so deep networks still train well.

EfficientNet-B0
2019 · 4.0 M weights

MBConv blocks with squeeze-and-excitation; depth, width and resolution are scaled together.

DenseNet-121
2017 · 7.0 M weights

Dense blocks: every layer receives the feature maps of all earlier layers in its block, so features are re-used.

MobileNetV3-Large
2019 · 3.0 M weights

Light inverted-residual blocks with hard-swish, designed to run fast on phones.

Each one first learned on 1.2 M ImageNet photos. Its 1000-class output layer is replaced by a new one for our classes, then all layers are fine-tuned on the retina photos.

How it works

1. Pre-process

Crop the black border, resize, Ben Graham contrast enhancement, shrink to 48 x 48.

2. 5-layer CNN

5 conv layers (32 to 512 filters) with BatchNorm, ReLU and max-pooling, trained from scratch. 1.57 M weights.

3. 4 pre-trained models

The 48 x 48 photo is enlarged to 128 x 128 and fed to ResNet-18, EfficientNet-B0, DenseNet-121 and MobileNetV3-Large, each fine-tuned (transfer learning).

4. Predict

Each model gives a probability per class; the photo and 3 mirrored copies are averaged. The page also shows how many models agree.
